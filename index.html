Appliance Leak Cleanup in Halton: A Homeowner's Checklist

Appliance leaks are often slow and hidden, which is why they spread further than people expect. Work down this list in order.

1. Make it safe

If you can do it safely, turn the appliance off and disconnect its power. Put towels down only where they will not create an electrical risk, and keep children and pets out of the wet area.

2. Stop the water at the right valve

Dishwasher: the supply valve is usually under the kitchen sink. Washing machine: two valves, hot and cold, behind the machine or on the wall box. Fridge with ice maker or water dispenser: a thin line often fed from under the sink or from a pipe in the basement ceiling. Water heater: the cold inlet valve at the top of the tank. If you cannot find or close the right valve, close the main shutoff, which in most Ontario houses is in the basement where the water line comes through the front wall near the meter.

3. Write down how long

Note when the leak may have started. A drip that ran for weeks behind a dishwasher is a different job from a hose that burst ten minutes ago, even if the puddle looks the same.

4. Check what got wet

Look at cabinet bases and toe-kicks, flooring seams and baseboards beside the appliance. If the laundry or kitchen is on an upper floor, check the ceiling below for stains or sagging, and stay out from under a sagging ceiling.

5. Take photos

Photograph the appliance, the failed part if you can see it, the water line and the neighbouring flooring and cabinetry before you clean up.

Prevention checklist

Swap rubber washing-machine hoses for braided steel ones and check them every year. Leave a little space behind the washer so hoses do not kink. Know the age of your water heater; tanks generally last around eight to twelve years, and a rusty base or water in the drain pan is an early warning. A drip pan and an inexpensive leak alarm under the water heater, washer and dishwasher can catch a slow leak early. Never run the washer or dishwasher while nobody is home.
